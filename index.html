The Overlooked Gap

The food system feeding the most children
is the one nobody is fixing.

East Africa's school feeding programmes serve over 52 million children daily. They are government-managed, centrally procured, and policy-governed — meaning a single ministerial directive can shift the dietary composition of millions of meals overnight.

"The vast majority of plant-forward NGO funding is directed at consumer behaviour change in Europe and North America. The result is a profound gap."

Meanwhile, institutional school meals in the Global South — the single largest food system feeding children — are completely overlooked by the organisations best positioned to change it.

A search of major philanthropic databases reveals no dedicated funding streams for plant-forward school meal policy in Sub-Saharan Africa. This is a genuine white space — high-impact, underfunded, and directly aligned with the food system transformation mission.

The PlantTable Model

Policy-first.
Evidence-driven.
Built to scale.

Rather than relying on individual consumer choice or expensive food product development, we change what millions of children eat every day by redesigning the institutional menu — replacing 2–3 animal-product meals per week with nutritionally equivalent legume, grain, and vegetable alternatives sourced locally. Legumes, sweet potato, and local greens are cheaper than meat per calorie, and they meet government nutritional guidelines.

1
Ministry Partnership
Approach county education departments with policy proposals backed by nutritional evidence, cost analysis, and pilot data.
2
Menu Redesign
Replace 2–3 animal-product meals per week with culturally familiar, locally sourced plant alternatives drawing on Kenya's rich culinary heritage.
3
Cook Training
Ministry-accredited short course covering plant-based techniques, nutritional equivalency, and food safety. Certified cooks receive a recognised qualification.
4
Measurement
Track meals replaced per child, nutritional outcomes (MUAC scores, micronutrient intake), and estimated carbon savings. All data published quarterly.
5
Open Replication
All policy templates, menus, curriculum, and frameworks published under open licence — any partner NGO in Uganda, Tanzania, or Ethiopia can adopt the full model.
